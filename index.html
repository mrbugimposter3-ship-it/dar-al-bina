❖ How to Build a Verse ❖

  1. Choose a Chamber — each Sūrah is a room of the great house.
  2. Choose a Verse — each Āyah is a stone to lay within it.
  3. Read the Āyah — its sacred letters are drawn from the text itself.
  4. Study the Master's Blueprint — the left panel shows the finished layout, generated from that verse's own letters. This is your guide.
  5. Gather Materials — the palette below holds every material this verse requires, shuffled so you must truly match, not merely count.
  6. Place Each Piece — click a material, then click a spot on Your Construction Site. Follow the Master's Instructions: “Place the Door (د) → Row 2, Col 3.”
  7. Check Your Work — green ✓ means true to the Blueprint; red ✗ means misplaced. Consult the Blueprint and try again.
  8. Complete the Chamber — build every verse of a Sūrah and that room of the house stands finished. Your progress is remembered.

❖ Why Every Verse Is Unique ❖

No two Āyahs in all the Quran produce the same structure. Each blueprint is woven from three strands that never repeat together:

  • 1. The Letter Fingerprint — every verse yields its own sequence of distinct letters, in the order they first appear. Like a fingerprint of ink, this sequence belongs to one verse alone. Two verses may share letters, but never the same sequence of them.
  • 2. The Deterministic Seed — the Sūrah and Āyah numbers forge a seed (Sūrah × 1000 + Āyah). This seed shuffles the layout positions. The same verse always yields the same blueprint — and no other verse can yield it, for no other verse carries its seed.
  • 3. The Woven Layout — the shuffled positions scatter the materials across the grid like stars in a constellation. Change one letter, one seed digit, and the entire constellation shifts.

Thus each blueprint is a seal: the verse's letters are the wax, the seed is the signet ring. Press them together and you get an impression that exists exactly once in all creation — as the verse itself does.

❖ The 28 Materials ❖

Each sacred letter is bound to an earthly material, all drawn close to nature — wood, stone, clay, water, and living green.
